# Roadmap and product concepts

Hold new features until two weeks of visitor numbers are in, then build the one thing the numbers point to. The site already covers water, mold and fire. What it lacks now is proof of what people use.

## Decision that shapes the rest

The site is a magnet for 9 Construction's partner relationships, not a software product. That is why there are no logins, no subscriptions and no ads, and why the "Talk to an expert" link matters more than another calculator.

## Next, in order

1. **Turn on analytics and set the expert link.** Both are in the launch checklist. Without them the site cannot show what works or produce a single contact.
2. **Owner review of the guides.** Field experience should correct anything the published sources got wrong.
3. **Switch on estimate comparison** and test it on two real estimates for the same loss.
4. **"Email me this result"** on the calculators. The cheapest way to build a contractor contact list. Needs an email-sending account.
5. **One new tool chosen from the numbers.** Candidates are below.

## Product concepts

### Equipment care log
A QR sticker on every scrubber, dehumidifier, air mover and meter. A tech scans it to log a filter change, a cleaning, a meter check, or "used on a Category 3 or mold job". Units that are overdue, or that left a contaminated job without being logged clean, are flagged. Each job gets an equipment record that backs up filter billing.

- **Why it is strong:** it solves the cross-contamination and care problem the guides describe, and shops would open it daily.
- **Why it waits:** it needs logins, storage and support. That is a second business. Revisit if the numbers show shops returning weekly.

### Drying log, next steps
The log is live and holds several jobs on one device. Possible additions: a dry-down chart per spot, photos per reading, and sharing a job between a tech's phone and the office. Sharing needs accounts, so it carries the same cost as the care log.

### Supplement request draft
Turns estimate-comparison results into a scope and pricing request. Fast to build on what exists. The wording must stay on scope and price and needs a lawyer's review first.

### Photo-to-scope draft
Jobsite photos in, a draft line-item list out. The most impressive idea and the hardest to make reliable.

### Smaller ideas
- Restoration glossary for new technicians
- Certifications guide (WRT, ASD, AMRT, FSRT, EPA RRP)
- New-technician training track with short quizzes
- Contractor directory and job board

## Deliberately not building, for now

| Idea | Reason |
| --- | --- |
| Subscriptions of any kind | Turns a trust-building site into a product that needs support |
| Directory and job board | Only works with a large audience, which does not exist yet |
| Training track, glossary, certifications guide | Each is reasonable; together they make a content site that needs constant upkeep |
| Policy coverage tools for contractors | Licensed public adjusting in several states |
| A PDF to ESX converter | Proprietary format, and Verisk sells its own |

## Name and domain notes

- restorationtools.org was chosen because the .com was taken and ".org" reads as a free resource.
- Considered and set aside: freeinsurancetools.com (wrong audience), ScopeCheck, SupplementDesk, RestoClaim.
- A subdomain of the 9 Construction site was the alternative for building that domain's search standing.
